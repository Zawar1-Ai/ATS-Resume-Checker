      What is an ATS, and why does it reject good people?

      An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) helps employers collect and manage applications. The features and screening rules vary between employers and products; some may search for terms from a job post, but there is no single scoring rule used by every ATS. This checker compares resume text with common job-post terms and checks a few basic resume signals. It can help you review your wording, but it cannot reproduce an employer's system or predict whether a recruiter will see your application.

      The 6 mistakes that get beginner resumes filtered out

      • One generic resume for every job. Each job post uses different words. Mirror the words of the specific post you're applying to — that's the whole game.
      • No Skills section. The ATS looks for a clear list of tools and skills. Burying "Excel" inside a paragraph is easy to miss; a Skills line is not.
      • Duties instead of results. "Responsible for answering emails" says what the job was. "Answered 60+ customer emails a day with a 98% satisfaction rating" says how well you did it — and adds keywords and numbers in one move.
      • Fancy formatting. Tables, text boxes, columns, headers/footers, and images can scramble your text when the ATS reads it. One column, standard headings, a normal font.
      • Missing contact details. No email or phone number on the resume itself means the recruiter can't reach you even if you pass the filter.
      • Wrong length. Under 200 words usually means missing content; over 1,000 usually means padding. For a beginner, one focused page — roughly 300–700 words — is the sweet spot.

      No experience? You still have keywords

      School projects, volunteer work, family business help, freelance gigs, and personal projects all count as places to use real skills. If you managed a class project's spreadsheet, that's data entry and Microsoft Excel. If you ran a small Instagram shop for a relative, that's customer service, order processing, and social media. Describe what you actually did, with the employer's words for it — that's honest, and it's how beginners pass the filter.

      What score do I need before applying?

      Use the score as a checklist, not a pass/fail cutoff. Review the category breakdown and missing keywords, then decide whether each suggestion accurately reflects your experience. Different employers and ATS products evaluate resumes differently.

      Should I copy the whole job description into my resume to get 100?

      No. Keyword stuffing is obvious to recruiters and can get you rejected at the human stage. Add the keywords you genuinely have, in sentences that describe real work you did.

      What file format should I send to employers?

      Follow the job post: if it asks for PDF, send PDF; if it doesn't say, a simple .docx or PDF from a one-column document both read well. Avoid Canva-style multi-column designs for ATS applications — they look great and parse badly.
